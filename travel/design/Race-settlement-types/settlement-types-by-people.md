# Settlement Types By People

This file connects the major peoples to the kinds of settlements they build.

The goal is:

- make settlements feel inhabited by distinct societies
- avoid generic economy-only town design
- support map planning, diplomacy, POI clustering, and warfare

Each settlement should later be defined by:

- people
- faith lean
- material culture
- economic role
- political stance
- military posture

## Human Settlements

Humans should have the widest variety of settlement forms because they are the most fragmented people.

### Coast Clans

- `Harbor Village`
  - fishing, small trade, boat repair, salt stores
  - open to barter, wary of strangers
  - nearby POIs:
    - wreck sites
    - tide caves
    - fish camps
    - smuggler caches

- `Raid Haven`
  - rough coastal hold with stronger war culture
  - more suspicious, more martial
  - nearby POIs:
    - hidden coves
    - beacons
    - stolen caches
    - watch posts

### Holdfolk

- `Timber Village`
  - woodcutting, trapping, storehouses, small farms
  - practical and communal
  - nearby POIs:
    - logging camps
    - hunter lean-tos
    - beast dens
    - old shrines

- `Hill Hold`
  - stone walls, stock yards, defended houses
  - slower to trust, stronger local authority
  - nearby POIs:
    - quarries
    - old towers
    - blocked passes
    - burial sites

- `Market Hold`
  - inland trade village, mixed shrines, local storage
  - more political and useful for diplomacy
  - nearby POIs:
    - road shrines
    - abandoned wagons
    - toll posts
    - camp clearings

### Ashbound

- `Ash Hamlet`
  - burned-country village with harsh rites and fear-heavy culture
  - outsiders are watched closely
  - nearby POIs:
    - ash pits
    - blackened shrines
    - grave circles
    - abandoned huts

- `Rite Hold`
  - stronger Ashbound center built around ritual authority
  - can act as a dark political or religious node
  - nearby POIs:
    - sacrificial grounds
    - torch lines
    - bone posts
    - guarded ash stores

## Elven Settlements

Elven settlements should feel deliberate, old, and spatially restrained.

### Typical Forms

- `Moon Grove`
  - sacred woodland settlement
  - ritual-focused, hidden, defensive in indirect ways
  - nearby POIs:
    - standing stones
    - hidden paths
    - memory pools
    - ward circles

- `High Hall`
  - elevated or secluded seat of local authority
  - old law, old grudges, controlled access
  - nearby POIs:
    - watch groves
    - oath markers
    - sealed ruins
    - ranger posts

- `Border Refuge`
  - smaller settlement on contested edges
  - more exposed, more militarized
  - nearby POIs:
    - scout nests
    - warning cairns
    - ambush trails
    - old battle sites

## Dwarven Settlements

Dwarven settlements should feel engineered, compact, and meant to last.

### Typical Forms

- `Hill Keep`
  - stone stronghold with storage and oath authority
  - hard to assault, slow to negotiate
  - nearby POIs:
    - quarry cuts
    - gate shrines
    - old walls
    - collapsed tunnels

- `Forge Town`
  - craft-heavy settlement centered on production
  - trade value high, military discipline steady
  - nearby POIs:
    - charcoal pits
    - ore cuts
    - smith camps
    - guarded supply yards

- `Mine Hall`
  - labor settlement built around extraction
  - dangerous but economically important
  - nearby POIs:
    - shaft entrances
    - spoil fields
    - cave beasts
    - sealed chambers

## Orc Settlements

Orc settlements should feel pressured, martial, and built around control rather than comfort.

### Typical Forms

- `War Camp`
  - mobile or semi-permanent raiding center
  - direct military pressure on nearby regions
  - nearby POIs:
    - raider trails
    - trophy poles
    - beast pens
    - captured supply pits

- `Frontier Fort`
  - stronger border settlement built to dominate a route
  - aggressive, territorial, difficult diplomacy
  - nearby POIs:
    - patrol lines
    - siege stockpiles
    - burned farms
    - challenge grounds

- `Clan Hold`
  - larger, more stable orc settlement
  - stronger internal hierarchy and stronger leaders
  - nearby POIs:
    - proving circles
    - war shrines
    - smith fires
    - captive camps

## Political Stance Layer

Settlement type should not decide attitude by itself.

Any settlement should also carry a stance such as:

- `open`
- `wary`
- `hostile`
- `fractured`
- `trade-minded`
- `expansionist`
- `isolated`

This keeps the same settlement type reusable.

Example:

- one Harbor Village can be trade-minded
- another can be raider-allied and hostile

## Map Use

When placing settlements on the larger regional map, use this pattern:

- major settlement hub
- 3 to 5 local POIs around it
- 1 to 2 farther satellite POIs
- shared route pressure with neighboring hubs

That makes regions feel socially distinct rather than just economically distinct.

## Recommended Early Regional Mix

For a first larger region map, a good mix would be:

- 1 player home settlement
- 1 Coast Clan harbor village
- 1 Holdfolk timber village or hill hold
- 1 Ashbound hamlet or rite hold
- 1 dwarven hill keep or forge town
- 1 orc frontier fort

Elven settlements can come slightly later if you want the map to begin with stronger human frontier tension.
