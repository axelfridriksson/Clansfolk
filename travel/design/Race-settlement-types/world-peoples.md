# World Peoples

This file defines the major peoples of the world at a high level before settlement politics, map expansion, and detailed pantheons are locked.

The goal is:

- keep each people distinct in material culture, faith, and warfare
- avoid flat fantasy race stereotypes
- give each settlement a clear social identity
- support later diplomacy, conflict, and map-region design

## Humans

Humans are the broadest and most internally divided people.

Core identity:

- adaptive
- local
- politically fragmented
- practical
- contradictory

Humans should feel like many overlapping communities rather than one unified civilization.

Human branches:

- `Coast Clans`
  - fishers, sailors, raiders, traders
  - pragmatic and mobile
  - strongest along coastlines and river mouths

- `Holdfolk`
  - inland farmers, timber settlers, hill communities
  - rooted, communal, work-driven
  - strongest in villages, clearings, and defended holdings

- `Ashbound`
  - harsh humans of burned country and blighted marches
  - feared by other humans
  - more punitive, rite-heavy, and severe

Humans use the broadest religious structure and tolerate the most belief overlap.

## Elves

Elves should feel old, controlled, and memory-heavy rather than simply elegant.

Core identity:

- long memory
- ritual precision
- continuity
- distance
- old law

They should feel like a people who remember too much and forgive very little.

How they live:

- hidden groves
- old halls
- elevated paths
- sacred clearings
- carefully shaped environments

How they fight:

- disciplined
- precise
- scout-heavy
- ranged pressure
- ritual wardens and oath-bound defenders

How they worship:

- fewer divine powers than humans
- deeper, more formal rites
- devotion tied to memory, moonlight, silence, old vows, and guarded places

World function:

- ancient politics
- hidden paths
- old grudges
- difficult alliances

## Dwarves

Dwarves should feel defined by endurance, craft, oath, and structure.

Core identity:

- endurance
- oath
- labor
- hierarchy
- record-keeping

They should feel compact, organized, and hard to move once committed.

How they live:

- hill keeps
- stone halls
- terraced strongholds
- mine-settlements
- forge communities

How they fight:

- tough
- slower
- shield-heavy
- disciplined infantry
- engineers and builders later

How they worship:

- formal and institutional
- centered on forge, stone, ancestry, and sworn word
- fewer gods, stronger systems

World function:

- stable trade
- hard alliances
- hard grudges
- strongholds that shape routes and war

## Orcs

Orcs should be a real people, not a mindless enemy type.

Core identity:

- force
- survival
- immediacy
- dominance
- harsh honor

They should feel like a people shaped by pressure, conflict, and hard living.

How they live:

- war camps
- rough hill forts
- mobile holdings
- burned-border settlements
- places built for pressure, not comfort

How they fight:

- aggressive
- morale-driven
- strong raiders
- shock troops
- war-leaders with strong personal pull

How they worship:

- direct and severe
- tied to war, hunger, storm, conquest, blood-price, and fate-through-conflict
- fewer divine powers, stronger demand for proof

World function:

- frontier pressure
- raids
- military expansion
- direct conflict with weaker settlements

## Belief Structure Across Peoples

The world works better if belief is structured differently by people rather than giving everyone the same religious behavior.

- `Humans`
  - many gods
  - many local cults
  - mixed devotion
  - belief is messy and flexible

- `Elves`
  - fewer gods
  - exact rites
  - belief is continuity and memory

- `Dwarves`
  - fewer gods
  - formal worship
  - belief is oath and order

- `Orcs`
  - fewer gods
  - direct cult practice
  - belief is force, survival, and proof

## Map Use

This structure exists to shape the regional map.

Settlements should later be built from:

- people
- faith lean
- economic role
- political stance
- military style

So instead of generic labels like:

- wood town
- war town
- stone town

you get places like:

- a Coast Clan harbor
- a Holdfolk timber village
- an Ashbound shrine-settlement
- a dwarven hill keep
- an elven moon-grove
- an orc frontier fort

That is the level of distinction the world needs before the map grows larger.
