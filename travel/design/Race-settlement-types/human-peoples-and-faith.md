# Human Peoples And Faith

## Human Peoples

Three broad human branches:

- `Coast Clans`
  - fishers, sailors, raiders, traders
  - practical, mobile, clan-led
  - strongest along shore and river mouths
  - religion is mixed and local

- `Holdfolk`
  - inland farmers, timber settlers, hill communities
  - steadier, more rooted, more communal
  - strongest around defended villages and working land
  - religion is seasonal, local, and kin-centered

- `Ashbound`
  - harsh humans shaped by burned land, rites, and fear
  - feared by other humans
  - strongest in blighted marches, ash fields, ruined borderlands
  - religion is severe, punitive, and death-marked

This gives the world a darker human group without flattening all humans into the same tone.

## Human Divine Forces

Humans use seven broad divine powers. These should not be treated as clean, fixed pantheon entries everywhere. Different settlements can use different names, rites, and interpretations.

- `The Hearth-Mother`
  - home, warmth, kin, shelter, birth, keeping people alive
  - strongest with Holdfolk and ordinary village life

- `The Storm-Father`
  - war, trial, weather, risk, strength, sea danger
  - strong among Coast Clans and warriors

- `The Tide-Walker`
  - travel, distance, trade, luck, crossing, unknown shores
  - mostly coastal worship, but also travelers inland

- `The Ash Veil`
  - death, memory, remains, loss, omen, the dead not fully gone
  - widely feared, often respected, central to Ashbound rites

- `The Green Hand`
  - beasts, food, herds, field luck, growth, hunting fortune
  - strongest with Holdfolk and frontier villages

- `The Forge-Warden`
  - craft, building, discipline, making, repair, useful order
  - worshiped by builders, smiths, and organized settlements

- `The Night Lantern`
  - secrecy, omen, wandering, hidden knowledge, dangerous truth
  - cults are scattered, often mistrusted, but persistent

## How Human Religion Works

Humans should not default to exclusive devotion.

They should:

- keep multiple household devotions
- favor one god in crisis
- blend village customs with clan practice
- disagree constantly about which rites matter most

Example:

- keep a Hearth-Mother shrine at home
- invoke Storm-Father before war
- make offerings to Tide-Walker before travel
- bury the dead under rites of the Ash Veil

This keeps human belief broad, practical, and inconsistent in a way that fits the setting.

## The Ashbound

The Ashbound are the darker human branch because they do not keep the wider balance of human religion. They overcommit to a harsher structure of belief.

They prioritize:

- `The Ash Veil`
- `The Night Lantern`
- harsher readings of `The Storm-Father`

Their worship should feel like:

- memory through suffering
- power through endurance
- truth through fear
- survival through sacrifice

They are not just evil for flavor. Their worldview is that soft people die, forget, and deserve to be ruled by harder hands.

## Subgroup Religious Lean

- `Coast Clans`
  - Tide-Walker
  - Storm-Father
  - Hearth-Mother

- `Holdfolk`
  - Hearth-Mother
  - Green Hand
  - Forge-Warden

- `Ashbound`
  - Ash Veil
  - Night Lantern
  - Storm-Father in harsher form

## World Use

This structure lets human settlements differ without inventing a separate race every time.

Examples:

- one coastal clan-village might center on Tide-Walker and Storm-Father
- one inland hold might center on Hearth-Mother and Green Hand
- one ash settlement might center on Ash Veil and Night Lantern

This is enough to drive:

- settlement identity
- shrine and rite visuals
- diplomacy
- local hostility
- encounter flavor
- map POI themes
- leader interaction with human groups
